5 OSINT Techniques Every Beginner Should Know

← Back to Blog
Cybersecurity
5 min read

Open-Source Intelligence (OSINT) is one of the first real skills you'll build in cybersecurity — and one of the most useful. Before you scan a system or test a login form, you gather information. Here are five techniques worth learning early.

1. WHOIS Lookups

Every domain name has registration data attached to it — who registered it, when, and often which registrar and name servers it uses. A WHOIS lookup is usually the first step in understanding who or what you're looking at online.

It won't always reveal a real name (privacy protection services hide a lot of this now), but it tells you a domain's age, registrar, and hosting patterns — useful signals on their own.

2. Google Dorking

Google Dorking means using advanced search operators to find information that isn't obvious from a normal search. Operators like site:, filetype:, and intitle: let you narrow results dramatically.

  • site:example.com filetype:pdf — find PDFs hosted on a specific domain
  • intitle:"index of" — find exposed directory listings
  • site:example.com inurl:admin — find admin-related pages on a domain

3. DNS Reconnaissance

DNS records reveal more than most people expect — mail servers, subdomains, and sometimes internal infrastructure hints. Tools like dig and nslookup (both available on Linux) let you pull this information directly from the command line.

Subdomain enumeration in particular often surfaces forgotten staging sites or admin panels that were never meant to be public-facing.

4. Social Media & Public Profiles

A huge amount of useful information sits in plain sight on LinkedIn, GitHub, and company "About" pages — employee names, job titles, tech stacks mentioned in job postings, and more. This is often how real attackers (and real penetration testers) build a picture of an organization before touching a single system.

5. Metadata in Public Documents

PDFs, Word documents, and images often carry metadata — author names, software versions, sometimes even internal file paths. Tools like exiftool can pull this out of publicly available files in seconds, and it's a step people frequently forget to clean before publishing documents online.

A Word on Ethics

OSINT is legal because it only touches publicly available information — but that doesn't mean anything goes. Always have explicit permission before gathering intelligence on a real target, and never use these skills to harass or stalk someone.
